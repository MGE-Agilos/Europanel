Before you start

Open the app with no selections active. Say in one sentence what they are about to see: a questionnaire the sector already fills in, turned into something the federation can argue with. Then work the eight moves in order — each one answers a question the room is already holding.

MOVE 01 Sheet 1 · Panel coverage
DoNothing yet. Leave every filter clear.

Establish who is in the room before showing any number

Representativeness is the first thing a regulator attacks, so it is the first thing the dashboard answers. The coverage table breaks down by country exactly the way EPF's own Annual Report does — sites, companies, submissions, declared production, completion rate.

The bar chart is deliberately uneven: seven sites in Germany, six in Poland, five in Italy, and thirteen countries carrying a single site each. That shape matters. It is where the panel is thin, not where it is thick, that determines what the federation can and cannot claim.

“Before any emission figure, here is precisely how much of the sector is behind it — and where the gaps are.”

One site reports 48.6 mg/Nm³ where the next highest in the whole panel is 18.7 — more than two and a half times the runner-up and roughly seven times the sector median. Nothing in the database flags it; it is found statistically, in two clicks.

“If this reached the JRC unchallenged, it would sit in the sector's evidence base as a real number. We would rather find it ourselves and go back to the operator.”

MOVE 06 Any sheet
DoWith that single site still selected, point at the median KPI — it has gone blank.

The confidentiality guard is in the measure, not in a promise

Sites in view1
Median shown—

Every headline statistic is written as If(Count(DISTINCT plant) >= 3, …). Narrow the selection below three contributing sites and the figure suppresses itself. A trade association pooling competitors' data cannot rely on users remembering a rule; the rule is compiled into the metric.

“You can hand this to any member. It will not show them a number that belongs to one competitor — it physically cannot.”

Both medians fall — formaldehyde 8%, dust 9% — but the panel is not the same panel. It grew from 31 reporting sites to 55 as the federation recruited, and 2025 shows 45 because the campaign is still open and thirteen sites have not finished their return. Anyone in the room can therefore ask the obvious question: did the sector improve, or did cleaner members simply join later?

That question is the point of this move. The site count is on the same chart as the trend, deliberately, so the question gets asked in the room rather than in a written objection three months later.

The answer is one selection away. Restrict the panel to the 27 sites that reported in all five campaigns and the improvement survives, smaller and less tidy:

Formaldehyde down 6%, dust down 7%, on the same plants throughout — with a rise in 2024 that a five-point series is entitled to have. Two of the 27 are missing from 2025 because their return is still a draft. A trend this shallow is what real panel data looks like; a clean monotonic fall across five years would be the thing to distrust.

“The panel grew from 31 sites to 58, so I am not going to claim a 9% improvement off that chart. On the plants that reported every single year, it is 6 to 7% — and that number I will defend.”

Closing ask

Everything shown runs on the questionnaire EPF already has. What turns it into this is a defined collection cycle, a stable site register, and the discipline of asking for the same fields each round. The dashboard is the easy part; the panel is the asset.

Say these out loud before anyone asks

  • The data is entirely synthetic. 59 fictional sites across 27 countries, generated to be realistic in shape and magnitude. No operator data appears anywhere, which is exactly the commitment made to EPF in writing. Nothing in the panel is a real filing.
  • The panel size is uneven on purpose. Sites per country run from seven down to one, sites per company from four down to one, and each site has a year in which it joined the panel — a few also skipped a campaign. That is why no aggregate here divides evenly, and why move 07 has to be careful about what the trend is measured on.
  • The circularity series has no direction. Recycled-wood share reads 16.4, 15.8, 17.3, 15.6 and 16.2 across the five campaigns — it wanders inside a point and a half because the generator draws the furnish mix afresh each year rather than drifting it. Present it as a level, not a trend; the up-down-up on sheet 6 is noise and someone will read it as a story.
  • Plywood is declared under “Other”. The questionnaire's product list offers OSB, particleboard, MDF, HDF, softboard, hardboard and MBH — but no plywood, though EPF counts it among the six panels it represents. Worth raising as a questionnaire fix, not a dashboard one.
  • Permit limits are free text. Operators write “20”, “<= 20” or “20 (daily average)”, so the exceedance measure parses the leading number. A limit written with its unit would need a sturdier parse before this figure is used in anger.
  • Concentrations are as reported. No normalisation to a common oxygen reference or moisture basis has been applied. For a real BREF submission that conversion has to happen before any cross-site comparison — the Technical WG will ask.
