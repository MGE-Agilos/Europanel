1. WHAT THE PROJECT IS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

EuroPanel is a data-collection and analysis chain built for the European Panel
Federation (EPF), the trade association of the wood-based panel industry in
Europe. EPF represents producers of particleboard (PB), MDF, HDF, OSB,
softboard (SB), hardboard (HB) and plywood.

The purpose. European industrial installations are regulated under the
Industrial Emissions Directive. For each sector the European Commission
publishes a BREF — a Best Available Techniques reference document — and its
BAT conclusions carry BAT-AELs, emission levels that installations must meet.
Those levels are negotiated in a Technical Working Group, on evidence. A
federation that arrives without its own data argues against the Commission's
numbers with opinion. A federation that arrives with a panel of member
measurements argues with evidence.

So the chain is: a questionnaire members fill in → a database → a Qlik Sense
dashboard that turns the answers into the shapes a BREF negotiation actually
uses (distributions, percentiles, per-emission-point splits, trends).

Two audiences, and they want different things:
  · The Environmental & Technical Working Group wants to know where the sector
    sits and which candidate limits exclude whom.
  · An individual operator wants to know where it sits inside the sector,
    without its name being visible to competitors.

That second need is why anonymity appears throughout the design rather than as
a policy note. See section 5.

2. THE QUESTIONNAIRE (the web form)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A static web application — plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript, no build
step, no framework — served from GitHub Pages. The backend is Supabase
(hosted PostgreSQL plus authentication). One operator account per production
site; each site fills in one questionnaire per collection campaign.

Thirteen pages. Twelve carry data, the thirteenth is Review & Submit:

   0  Cover & Contact        who is answering, plus the Member-State
                             representative for the Technical Working Group
   1  General Information    site identity, products made, quantities,
                             site activities
   2  Plant Layout           storage, wood preparation, diffuse-dust sources
   3  Raw Materials          furnish composition in %, resins, hardeners,
                             additives
   4  Energy Production      combustion units, rated thermal input, fuel mix
   5  Dryer & Press Line     dryer and press technology, moisture in and out
   6  Air Abatement          abatement techniques, what they treat, flows
   7  Air Emissions          emission points (stacks) and the pollutants
                             measured at each — the heart of the exercise
   8  Water Emissions        discharge points, parameters, receiving water
   9  Solid Residues         waste streams and where each one goes
  10  Water Consumption      process, cooling, steam, sanitary, other
  11  BAT Candidate          optional: a technique the site puts forward
  12  Review & Submit        completeness check, then submit

Pages 1 to 10 are required; page 11 is optional.

Three shapes of question recur, and they explain the whole data model:
  · A flat block — one answer per field per submission. Page 0's contacts, for
    example, or page 11's BAT candidate.
  · A repeating group — the operator adds as many instances as the site has.
    Emission points, combustion units, dryers, presses, waste streams. Each
    instance carries an index (idx).
  · A coded list — a fixed list of items from a reference list, each with a
    value. Fuel types with their percentage, pollutants with their
    concentration, raw materials with their share of the furnish.

Some coded lists hang off a repeating group rather than off the submission:
the pollutants measured at emission point 2 belong to that point, not to the
site in general. That nesting is the only real complexity in the model.

Saving is per page, as the operator works. A submission is a draft until it is
submitted; a draft can be left half-finished and picked up later.

3. THE DATA MODEL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PostgreSQL, schema `europanel`. Four backbone tables plus 35 questionnaire
tables.

The backbone:
  companies     name, country
  plants        production site, belongs to a company, has a country
  cycles        a collection campaign: label, reference year, open and close
                dates, status (draft / open / closed / archived)
  submissions   one questionnaire return: which plant, which cycle, which
                user, status (draft / submitted), timestamps

Every questionnaire table hangs off `submissions` by submission_id, and
cascades on delete. The 35 tables follow the three shapes above:

  12 flat tables      one row per submission. The widest are bat_candidate
                      (26 columns), plant_layout_section (22), contacts (17).
   9 repeating groups one row per instance, carrying idx. plant_products,
                      resins, hardeners, combustion_units, dryers, presses,
                      abatement_techniques, emission_points,
                      waste_water_discharges, waste_streams.
  14 coded lists      one row per code, with its value. Five of them hang off
                      a repeating group instead of the submission:
                      combustion_unit_fuels, abatement_technique_sources,
                      abatement_technique_flows, emission_point_pollutants,
                      waste_water_pollutants, waste_water_sources.

A `ref_lists` table holds the reference lists — pollutant codes and their
labels and units, fuel types, raw-material categories, and so on. The key is
list_code + code, never code alone: the same code ("other", "toc") reappears
in several lists and matching on it alone would join unrelated rows.

One design note worth knowing, because it explains a lot of the code: the
questionnaire originally stored each page as a JSONB blob. It was migrated to
relational tables so that the dashboard could aggregate across sites without
parsing JSON at query time. A single manifest, `docs/fields.js`, describes
every table, its shape and its columns. The form's save logic, the database
schema and the Qlik load script are all generated from or checked against that
one manifest, so they cannot drift apart.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
4. THE QLIK SENSE APPLICATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Qlik Sense Cloud, app "EuroPanels".

How the data gets in. The load script is generated from the same manifest
(docs/fields.js) by a Node script in the repository. It is split into fifteen
files — a core file, the reference-list mapping, a small what-if file and one
file per questionnaire page — which live in the Qlik space's data files and are
assembled by Must_Include. Must_Include rather than Include on purpose: an
Include with a wrong path raises no error at all, so the reload would finish
"successfully" having read nothing and the app would simply look empty.

Data is read from Supabase over its REST API, paged 1000 rows at a time —
PostgREST caps a request at that, and asking for more returns fewer rows
without warning.

Reference labels are resolved at load time by ApplyMap rather than by joining a
shared ref_lists table. A shared table joined to fourteen coded-list tables
would link them all to each other through the middle of the model, creating
circular references and synthetic keys.

18 master dimensions, all plain fields, no expressions. The ones that matter:
Country, Company, Plant, Collection cycle, Reference year, Submission status,
Panel type, Pollutant, Emission point type, Resin type, Fuel, Abatement
technique, Measurement method, Waste destination, Raw material.

24 master measures. The ones that carry the argument:

  Plants in view          Count(DISTINCT plant_id)
                          Show this beside any aggregate. Representativeness
                          is the first thing a regulator questions.
  Median conc.            If(Count(DISTINCT plant_id) >= 3,
    (anonymity-safe)        Median(emission_point_pollutants_conc))
  P25 / P75 / P90         same guard, Fractile at 0.25 / 0.75 / 0.90
  Max concentration       same guard, Max
  Above permit limit      share of measurements above the permit limit stated
                          for that same pollutant
  Pollutant load          Sum of t/year — a low concentration on a very large
                          flow is not a small emission
  Annual production       Sum(plant_products_qty), the denominator for any
                          intensity measure
  Recycled wood share     average share of externally collected recycled wood
  Wood-based fuel share   share of combustion fuel that is wood, not fossil
  Waste diverted          share of solid waste going anywhere but landfill
    from landfill
  Water per m³ produced   normalised, so plants of different sizes compare
  Submitted rate          share of submissions submitted rather than draft

Six sheets:

  1. Panel coverage and data quality
     How much of the sector the data represents. KPIs for plants and
     companies, submissions and submitted rate; sites per country; sites per
     panel type; responses per reference year against sites reporting; a
     coverage table by country that mirrors EPF's own Annual Report.

  2. Emissions benchmark
     Where the sector sits, pollutant by pollutant: P25, median, P75 and P90
     in mg/Nm³ for fourteen pollutants; a candidate-AEL simulator showing how
     many sites would fail at 20, 15, 10 and 5 mg/Nm³; peak concentration by
     site; a statistics table that always shows the plant count beside the
     value.

  3. What drives emissions
     Concentrations split by emission point, panel type, resin and fuel — the
     evidence from which BAT conclusions are actually written.

  4. Abatement techniques in use
     Which techniques are installed, on how many sites, and what emissions
     look like where they are used. Evidence for whether a technique is
     genuinely "available" in the BAT sense.

  5. Circularity and resources
     Recycled wood in the furnish, wood-based fuel, waste diverted from
     landfill, water intensity — the indicators behind EPF's circular-economy
     positions.

  6. Trends across campaigns
     Five reference years, with the panel composition shown alongside so a
     trend is never read off a changing sample.

Odour (ouE/m³) and PCDD/PCDF (ng I-TEQ/Nm³) are excluded from the mass-
concentration charts. They are not mass concentrations; putting them on an
axis in mg/Nm³ would let an unrelated quantity set the scale. They are
excluded by a field computed in the load script, not by an expression hidden
inside a chart.

5. THE ANONYMITY GUARD
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Every headline statistic is written as:

    If(Count(DISTINCT plant_id) >= 3, <the calculation>)

Narrow a selection below three contributing sites and the figure suppresses
itself — it goes blank. A trade association pooling competitors' data cannot
rely on users remembering a rule, so the rule is compiled into the metric.

This is worth being precise about when asked:
  · It is a floor on the number of contributing SITES, not on the number of
    measurements.
  · It suppresses aggregates. Per-site charts, such as peak concentration by
    site, deliberately show their value — that is a federation-internal view
    and would sit behind section access in production.
  · It is not encryption or pseudonymisation. It is a disclosure control, and
    a determined user with enough selections could still narrow things down.
    Section access is the answer to that, and is not yet configured.

6. THE DEMONSTRATION DATASET
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Entirely synthetic. Company and site names are invented from place names and
common words. No operator data appears anywhere, which is the commitment made
to EPF in writing.

  59 production sites
  33 companies
  27 countries
  237 submissions
   5 collection campaigns, reference years 2021 to 2025 (2025 still open)
  ~27 400 database rows

It is deliberately uneven, because an even panel resembles no real industry:
  · Sites per country run from seven (Germany) down to one (thirteen
    countries have a single site). Poland has six, Italy five, France and
    Spain four.
  · Sites per company run from four down to one.
  · Each site has a year in which it joined the panel, so responses climb
    from 31 in 2021 to 58 in 2025. A few sites also skipped a campaign, the
    way real voluntary collection works.
  · Thirteen sites have left their 2025 return as a draft, so the submitted
    rate is 94.5% rather than 100%.
  · Annual production per site runs from 39 600 to 470 000 m³.

Five deliberate outliers are planted across five different reference years,
three to six times above the sector range. Nothing in the database flags
them — they have to be found statistically. The most visible is a formaldehyde
reading of 48.6 mg/Nm³ where the sector median is 7.0.

7. THE DEMONSTRATION WALKTHROUGH — EIGHT MOVES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The scripted run through the dashboard. Each move answers a question the room
is already holding. Figures below are live readings from the app.

MOVE 01 — Establish who is in the room before showing any number
  Sheet 1, no selections. 59 sites, 33 companies, 27 countries, 94% submitted.
  Representativeness is the first thing a regulator attacks, so it is the
  first thing answered. The bar chart is deliberately uneven; it is where the
  panel is thin, not where it is thick, that limits what can be claimed.

MOVE 02 — Show the sector as a distribution, never as an average
  Sheet 2, select Pollutant = PM. 58 sites, median 10.6, P75 13.5, P90 16.2
  mg/Nm³. "A mean would tell you 10.6. The useful number is that a tenth of
  the sector sits above 16."

MOVE 03 — Turn the distribution into a negotiating position
  Same sheet, the AEL simulator. Of 58 sites, a dust limit of 20 mg/Nm³ fails
  7 sites (12%); 15 fails 22 (38%); 10 fails 45 (78%); 5 fails 56 (97%).
  This is the move that changes the conversation: it converts member data
  into an answer to the only question a candidate limit really poses — who,
  concretely, cannot comply.

MOVE 04 — One limit cannot cover four different stacks
  Sheet 3, select Pollutant = Formaldehyde. Median mg/Nm³ by emission point:
  recycled-wood-fired dryer with abatement 9.88 (19 sites); sanding and
  cut-to-size dust filter 7.06 (19); press exhaust 6.97 (48); standard dryer
  stack 6.10 (39). A recycled-wood-fired dryer sits 62% above a standard one
  on the same pollutant. Averaging them produces a number describing no real
  installation — the technical argument for emission-point-specific
  conclusions.

MOVE 05 — Catch the bad measurement before Brussels does
  Sheet 2, still on formaldehyde, peak concentration by site. One site reads
  48.6 mg/Nm³ where the next highest in the panel is 18.7 and the sector
  median is 7.0. Nothing in the database flags it; it is found statistically,
  in two clicks. "We would rather find it ourselves and go back to the
  operator."

MOVE 06 — The confidentiality guard is in the measure, not in a promise
  Click that single site. The sector statistics go blank; the per-site peak
  still shows. "You can hand this to any member. It will not show them a
  number that belongs to one competitor — it physically cannot."

MOVE 07 — A falling trend, on a panel that grew underneath it
  Sheet 6, no selections. Formaldehyde falls 7.25 → 6.68 and dust 11.2 → 10.2
  across 2021-2025 — but the panel grew from 31 reporting sites to 55, so the
  obvious question is whether the sector improved or cleaner members joined
  later. The site count is on the same chart as the trend precisely so that
  question gets asked in the room rather than in a written objection three
  months later. Restricted to the 27 sites that reported in all five
  campaigns, the improvement survives, smaller: formaldehyde down 6%, dust
  down 7%, with a rise in 2024. A clean monotonic fall would be the thing to
  distrust.

MOVE 08 — The same questionnaire already answers the circular-economy file
  Sheet 5, no selections. Recycled wood 16.2%, wood-based fuel 64.5%, waste
  diverted from landfill 94.9%. Three figures out of the emissions
  questionnaire at no extra cost to the operator, mapping onto positions EPF
  already defends: cascading use, recycled-wood standards, the waste
  hierarchy applied to wood.

The closing ask: everything shown runs on the questionnaire EPF already has.
What turns it into this is a defined collection cycle, a stable site register,
and the discipline of asking for the same fields each round. The dashboard is
the easy part; the panel is the asset.

8. KNOWN LIMITS — say these before anyone asks
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

· Concentrations are as reported. No normalisation to a common oxygen
  reference or moisture basis has been applied. For a real BREF submission
  that conversion has to happen before any cross-site comparison, and the
  Technical Working Group will ask about it.

· Permit limits are free text. Operators write "20", "<= 20" or
  "20 (daily average)", so the exceedance measure parses the leading number.
  A limit written with its unit would need a sturdier parse before the figure
  is used in anger.

· Plywood has no option in the questionnaire. The product list offers OSB,
  particleboard, MDF, HDF, softboard, hardboard and MBH, though EPF counts
  plywood among the panels it represents. In the demo data it is declared
  under "Other". This is a questionnaire fix, not a dashboard one.

· The circularity series has no direction. Recycled-wood share reads 16.4,
  15.8, 17.3, 15.6 and 16.2 across the five campaigns — it wanders inside a
  point and a half because the data generator draws the furnish mix afresh
  each year rather than drifting it. Present it as a level, not a trend.

· Section access is not configured. Today anyone who can open the app sees
  every sheet. The three-site guard limits what aggregates reveal, but
  per-site views are visible. Production use needs section access.

· The panel is uneven by design, not by accident. Thirteen countries carry a
  single site. Any country-level figure for those rests on one site and is
  suppressed wherever the three-site guard applies.
